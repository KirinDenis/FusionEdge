FusionEdge Flasher

Put the FusionEdge internet radio firmware on your board from this page: pick your hardware, press the button, pick the board's port. Each build is made by GitHub Actions from the project's own files for that board, on every push.

It needs Chrome or Edge on a computer: they have Web Serial, the way for a page to reach a USB port. Phones, Firefox and Safari don't.

1. Pick your hardware

Firmware @VERSION@

Not every build here has been run on a board: the CrowPanel and the new screen on the boards with PSRAM have not, the two CYD-style boards have. The pins are the ones in the board's myoptions.h, linked on each card: check them against your wiring.

ESP32-S3 DevKitC-1 N16R8 with a 3.5" ILI9488

The project's main build. A 480x320 ILI9488 SPI display with FT6X36 capacitive touch, a PCM5102A DAC, one encoder, an SD card, an IR receiver and the QCC5124EL Bluetooth module. Pins: myoptions.h.

Guition JC3248W535

ESP32-S3 N16R8 with its own 3.5" 480x320 AXS15231B QSPI display and touch. A PCM5102A DAC, one encoder and an SD card. Pins: AXS15231B_options/myoptions.h.

4D Systems gen4-ESP32-35CT

ESP32-S3 N16R8 with its own 3.5" ILI9488 display, FocalTech capacitive touch and microSD. The I2S DAC on GPIO 38, 39 and 40. Bluetooth, IR and encoders are off. Pins: gen4-ESP32-35CT_options/myoptions.h.

ESP32-WROVER-E N16R8

The classic ESP32, not the S3. An ILI9488 SPI display, an I2S DAC and two encoders. Touch, SD, Bluetooth, DLNA and album covers are off. Pins: esp32-wrover-n16r8/myoptions.h.
Experimental, as the project says in its notes: the web page can be slow while it plays, and HTTPS streams may fail.

Elecrow CrowPanel Advance 2.8"

ESP32-S3 N16R8 with its own 2.8" 320x240 ST7789 IPS display, FT6336U capacitive touch, microSD and a speaker on its NS4168 amplifier: nothing to wire. Not the plain CrowPanel 2.8", which is a classic ESP32. In English. Pins: CrowPanel-Advance-2.8_options/myoptions.h.
New: a 320x240 layout, not run on a board yet. If a tap lands on the wrong side, switch the touch mirroring on the radio's web page.

ESP32-2432S028R, the 2.8" CYD

The "Cheap Yellow Display": a classic ESP32 with 4 MB of flash and no PSRAM, a 2.8" 320x240 ILI9341 display, XPT2046 resistive touch and a speaker socket. Plug a small speaker in: the sound comes from the ESP32's own DAC. In English. Pins: ESP32-2432S028R_options/myoptions.h.
New, and without PSRAM: a smaller screen of its own (player, station list, the stream's numbers, settings, touch calibration), MP3 streams over plain HTTP only, 8-bit mono sound, no SD card. The board with an ST7789 (marked 7789) is not this build.

LCDWIKI E32R35T, 3.5" ESP32-32E

A classic ESP32 with 4 MB of flash and no PSRAM, a 3.5" 480x320 ST7796 display, XPT2046 resistive touch, microSD and a speaker socket. Plug a small speaker in: the sound comes from the ESP32's own DAC. In English. Pins: E32R35T_options/myoptions.h.
New, and without PSRAM: a smaller screen of its own (player, station list, the stream's numbers, settings, touch calibration), MP3 streams over plain HTTP only, 8-bit mono sound.

The screen

A board with PSRAM has a choice on its card. FusionEdge's is the project's own screen. New is a different one, the same firmware otherwise: the player with the clock and a sound indicator that changes with a tap (bars, a scope, a radar), the station list, the stream's numbers with its last minute of traffic as a graph, and settings for the colours, the indicator, the brightness and turning the screen round. Swipe up or down for the volume, left or right for the next station. The two CYD-style boards have no PSRAM: they come with the new screen only. It has run on those two; on the boards with PSRAM it has not been run yet.

2. Before you press

  • INSTALL EVERYTHING writes the firmware and the radio's files: its web pages, fonts, pictures, the station list and the WiFi list. Use it on a new board, and for a new version. It takes several minutes: about 6 MB go over the cable.
  • It then asks whether to erase. Yes on a new board or one that had other firmware on it. No for an update: the radio's settings stay. The station list and the WiFi list still go back to the ones it came with, so export them from the radio's web page first and import them after.
  • UPDATE FIRMWARE ONLY leaves all the radio's files as they are, no to erase. The web pages then stay at their old version too, and the project asks for the matching files with a new version: when in doubt, INSTALL EVERYTHING.
  • No port in the list? The cable may only charge, or the USB chip's driver is missing (CH340, CP210x).
  • It won't connect? Hold BOOT, press and let go of RST, let go of BOOT, then press the button here again.
  • Close the flasher's window when it is done: one port, one program at a time.

3. After it starts

Give the radio your WiFi over the same cable. Close the flasher's window first, then send: the radio saves the network, restarts, joins it and this page shows its address. The name and the password go to the board on the cable and nowhere else; a network the radio knew before stays in its list.

  • The two CYD-style boards ask for a touch calibration on their first start: four crosses, a tap on each. Later again from the settings, or with the BOOT button.
  • The two CYD-style boards play through the 2-pin 1.25 mm speaker socket: a passive speaker of 8 ohms, 0.5 to 2 W. Not into an amplifier or a computer's input: both of its wires carry the sound, neither is ground.
  • Or without the cable: with no WiFi known the radio opens its own network, FusionEdge_AP, password 12345987. Join it, open http://192.168.4.1 and give the radio your WiFi there.
  • The radio speaks the language its myoptions.h sets: Hungarian on most boards, English on the CrowPanel and the two CYD-style boards. The new screen's own words are English on every board. For another language, build it yourself as the README says.
  • Later updates of the firmware alone can also go in through the radio's own web page, Update, with the firmware.bin from your board's card, under Files for esptool and other tools.
